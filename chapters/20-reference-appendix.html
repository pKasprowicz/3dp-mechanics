20.9 Further reading and resources

Books that reward a long shelf life (find them in a library or second-hand):

  • Machinery's Handbook (Oberg et al.): the workshop encyclopaedia of formulas, tables, threads, fits and gears.
  • Shigley's Mechanical Engineering Design (Budynas and Nisbett): the standard text on machine elements: shafts, gears, bearings, screws, springs, fatigue.
  • Design of Machinery (Norton): linkages, cams and kinematics, with a practical flavour.
  • Roark's Formulas for Stress and Strain: the lookup for beams, plates, stress concentration.
  • Compliant Mechanisms (Howell): the pseudo-rigid-body method and flexure design.
  • Mechanisms and Mechanical Devices Sourcebook (Chironis and Sclater) and 507 Mechanical Movements (Henry T. Brown, public domain): catalogues of mechanisms for inspiration.
  • Precision Machine Design (Slocum): exact constraint, kinematic couplings, error budgets.

On the web: the manufacturer wikis for your printer and slicer (the Bambu Lab wiki for your X2D and AMS), the knowledge bases of other printer makers (they contain careful material notes), the Cornell KMODDL collection of historical mechanism models, bearing and screw suppliers' catalogues (a free education in standard sizes), and the many hobbyist channels that test printed parts to destruction (search for test results on printed gears, threads and insert pull-out). Treat any single test as one data point.

20.10 A suggested learning path

  1. Print the fit coupon (chapter 4) in your two most-used materials; fill in your printer profile (chapter 18).
  2. Build a few small projects that each exercise a different joint: a hinge with a snap latch, a cam-driven automaton, a belt-driven slider, a gear train.
  3. Break things on purpose: load printed beams, hinges, snap-fits and screw holes until they fail; compare with the calculators.
  4. Start a notebook with the lessons, the settings that worked and the parameters you settled on.
  5. Return to this compendium whenever a new project hits an unfamiliar problem. Most are one of those in chapter 17.
